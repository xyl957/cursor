## The one-line thesis

The West is not running out of crude. It is running out of **middle distillate** into harvest and heating season, and the last three suppliers that could fill the hole — Russia, China, and the Gulf — are being taken off the board in the same week. The Gulf Coast crack is already in the price of VLO and MPC. The next print is **New York Harbor heating oil** and the ships that move the last diesel barrels to Europe.

## Why crude is the wrong expression

Brent is ~$107 and WTI is ~$103 on 16 September. That is loud. It is also the wrong instrument.

- The ULSD crack printed an intraday record of **$108.02/bbl** on 3 September. Diesel is paying a scarcity premium **on top of** crude.
- Stillwater Associates: diesel’s correlation with crude has collapsed (R² from ~0.70 to ~0.40). A dollar of crude now barely moves diesel. The diesel price is being set by **who can go without**, not by the wellhead.
- US crude production is **13.9 million b/d**. The Baker Hughes oil-rig count rose. Primary Vision’s frac-spread count **fell for a fourth week** to 178, the lowest since May. The constraint is not the Permian. It is **cokers, hydrocrackers, and tanks**.
- Continental Resources’ Ayacucho 2 MOU with PDVSA (30 billion barrels resource in place, 100% WI once the CPP is signed) is a 2028–2030 heavy-oil story, and Continental is **private**. Chevron’s $7 billion Orinoco spend is the public Venezuela vehicle. Extra-heavy Orinoco is the **right quality** for diesel yield — but only inside a coking refinery that already exists. That is a feedstock tailwind for USGC/East Coast complexity, not a reason to buy WTI.

Goldman Sachs, on 31 August, more than doubled its diesel-margin forecast to **$63/bbl US and $49/bbl Europe vs Brent through end-2027**. The bank is saying the bottleneck lasts past any ceasefire headline. BMI’s extended-conflict case puts 2026 global diesel at **$111/bbl**.

If you own crude here, you own a market that still has US barrels, Venezuelan barrels in the pipeline, and Saudi STS workarounds. If you own distillate capacity, you own the thing that cannot be replaced this winter.

## The next print: NYH heating oil

The underpriced signal is sitting in a boring spread.

Stillwater: the NY Harbor vs Gulf Coast ULSD differential collapsed from **19.9 cpg in February to 4.0 cpg in July**, against a 2024–25 average of 8.3 cpg. Four cents does not pay to barge diesel from Houston to New York. So the barrel loads for **Europe and Latin America** instead. EIA-week math: US refiners are sending roughly a **third** of distillate to the export dock, ~400 kb/d above the pre-Hormuz run-rate, and still drawing inventories.

That cannot survive a Northeast heating season.

- Distillate is diesel **and** heating oil. The >500 ppm residual is only ~6 million barrels of the US tank. The rest is the same molecule the trucks and the oil burners compete for.
- Distillate stocks are **13% below** the five-year average in mid-September. EIA’s STEO still has them going **sub-100 million barrels** and staying depressed through much of 2027.
- The curve is in steep backwardation (October ULSD ~25 cpg over November; HO 2027 more than $1/gal cheaper). Backwardation **punishes storage**. Nobody is building the winter tank. Germany’s gas storage is 56% for the same reason: prompt is too expensive to inject.
- Harvest is now. Heating demand starts in October. PBF’s own East Coast 2-1-1 is Dated Brent / RBOB / ULSD — it is a **NYH** number, not a Gulf number.

The next price that has to move is New York Harbor, not Port Arthur. When NYH finally pays up to keep barrels domestic, East Coast complex refiners collect it. If Washington bans exports, NYH still pays up — and Europe goes into a worse squeeze, which is the satellite book.

Do **not** buy HO futures to express this. The backwardation will eat a long on the roll. Buy the plant that sells the physical barrel into the squeeze.

## Primary: PBF Energy (NYSE: PBF)

**Price:** $75.93 (16 September 2026)
**Why this name, not VLO:**

1. **It sits on the next print.** Delaware City (180 kb/d, Nelson 13.6) and Paulsboro on the Delaware River are an East Coast heavy-sour system that makes heating oil, ULSD, and jet into PADD 1. Toledo supplies the Midwest freight market. Chalmette is the Gulf export/coker. This is the listed heating-oil refiner, not a Gulf export machine that has already been bid.

2. **Torque is still in the multiple.** Q2 2026 adjusted fully-converted EPS was **$6.22**. At $76, the stock is a mid-single-digit multiple of a single strong quarter annualized — versus VLO at $403 after a 140% run. PBF also cut net debt by **$1.4 billion** in Q2. Martinez is back at full rates after the February 2025 fire. The 2025 distressed-refiner discount has not fully left the stock.

3. **Heavy crude is the right feedstock.** The barrels being lost (Gulf medium/sour, Russian Urals/diesel) are the barrels that make distillate. Venezuelan extra-heavy, if Chevron and Continental actually move it, is a **coker** diet. Delaware City and Chalmette are built for that slate. A Continental-PDVSA MOU does not put oil in tanks this winter, but it is the correct direction of travel for PBF’s complexity, not for a Bakken producer.

4. **An export ban is not a kill shot here the way it is for a pure USGC exporter.** A Thune-style diesel export ban would smash the USGC-Europe arb (bad for VLO Port Arthur, bad for STNG) and **raise** the premium on remaining domestic barrels in PADD 1. PBF still has Chalmette export exposure, so it is not immune. It is better situated than Valero if Washington panics.

### Size it for the turnarounds

PBF’s own 2026 guidance puts **three Q4 turnarounds** on the calendar: Chalmette crude/coker (50–55 days), Paulsboro crude (30–35 days), Toledo FCC (50–60 days). That is the main idiosyncratic risk. Paulsboro down into heating season cuts PBF’s own barrels **and** tightens NYH for everyone else. Delaware City still runs. Treat PBF as a **high-torque, high-idiosyncratic-risk** expression, not a 5% index weight. If the turnaround calendar is a deal-breaker, the satellite (STNG) and the European gasoil future are the cleaner winter instruments.

## What has to be true / what kills it

**Bull case holds if two or more of these stick into November:**

1. Hormuz 10-day average stays in single digits.
2. Russian diesel ban holds past 31 October, or Kirishi/NORSI stay down.
3. China actually cuts Q4 product exports toward 1.2 million tonnes/month.
4. East-West pipeline is weeks, not days.
5. US distillate does not rebuild toward the five-year mean.
6. NYH–USGC ULSD spread re-expands toward (or through) the old 8–20 cpg range as heating demand hits.

**Kill the PBF long if:**

- EIA distillate rebuilds several weeks in a row toward the five-year band **and** the diesel crack mean-reverts below ~$40/bbl while crude holds up (true demand destruction / recession).
- Martinez-class operational failure, or Q4 turnarounds slip and strand the name through peak cracks.
- A ceasefire plus a visible Hormuz reopening **and** a Russian export restart in the same window. One of those is not enough; the stack is three sources.

**Kill the STNG long if:** Congress or the White House moves from “open to exploring” to a real diesel export restriction.
